Key facts about a drain snake and the job

A plumber may first assess the fixture and ask about the blockage, then choose an appropriate method based on what they find. With snaking, the tool is guided into the drain and worked against the obstruction. The details vary by drain access and the nature of the clog, so ask the plumber to describe the planned approach.

  • What it covers: Snaking can address some obstructions in a drain line. It may not resolve a damaged pipe or a blockage that needs another method.
  • Typical timing: A simple, accessible clog may take less time than a difficult-to-reach or recurring problem. Ask for a time estimate after the plumber understands the situation.
  • What affects cost: The drain’s location, access, obstruction, time involved, and any additional diagnosis or work can affect the charge. Request the cost details before authorizing service.
  • What to report: Which drain is slow or blocked, whether other fixtures are affected, and whether the problem has returned.
